## Rules

Zombie Shark is a third playable character in every candidate region. Shared growth, maximum 30 m length, food efficiency, survival, contact bodies, regional objectives and run timing remain unchanged. Starts are 3 m except Mariana's configured 15 m. Base sprint speed is 32 m/s; Orca retains its 30% advantage at 41.6 m/s.

Undead Fission requires at least 5 m and health/stamina/hunger each at least 50. One atomic successful cast subtracts 50 from all three. Insufficient resources, terminal state or cooldown reject without payment. Invulnerability does not waive the cost. Exactly 50 health can be paid, causing death; the ready button and Guide explicitly warn about this lethal sacrifice.

Lifetime and cooldown both end 60 active-play seconds after activation. There is at most one companion; pause, loading and hidden-page suspension do not advance its timer. At exactly 5 m, activation is legal, but no positive-sized prey fits the feeding limit yet. The companion has 65% of the owner's current visible length. It orbits, searches every 0.25 seconds within 32 world units of the owner, and returns when separated by more than 64 units. Cruise pursuit is 20 m/s; orbit/return can use 48 m/s to keep pace with a sprinting owner.

Its capture limit is the owner's current length minus 5 m, inclusive. A 20 m owner allows 15 m prey; the companion's visible size and Frenzy do not change this rule. Lords and vehicles are excluded. Pursuit uses the real solid-world query and continuous multisphere movement. Capture requires the moving visible mouth and moving prey to meet within the existing capture tolerance, with line-of-sight checks. The owner's direct meal is processed first; retired prey cannot be charged twice.

Shared food, healing-before-growth, mass, length and meal count credit the owner. This character's companion also restores stamina by effective meal nutrition, capped at 100; undersized-food efficiency remains applicable. Ordinary wildlife and human retirement retain existing respawn rules. New growth is constrained against solids in the same frame. The separate swallow transition restores borrowed prey on reset, and its hidden cooldown prevents repeated meals.

At expiry, the shark disappears in a short corpse-cloud burst and water-pressure sound. It causes no area damage or extra loot. Home, death, victory, time limit and destination change clear the companion and reset its state. Its cached mesh, shared materials/geometry, bounded swallow transition, existing sprite pool and central frame clock prevent additional render loops or per-summon scene growth.

## Visual and sound approach

This is an original undead shark, inspired by exposed skeletal gaps, withered tissue and damaged fins described in Disney's [official VFX interview](https://d23.com/the-visual-effects-that-tell-the-tales/), rather than a copied film model. Its continuous shark profile has irregular open flank wounds, cartilage ribs and vertebrae, damaged cheek/jaw, interrupted teeth, notched dorsal and asymmetrical pectoral fins. Independent axial bones provide lateral tail propulsion; the jaw responds to real feeding. Guide, player and companion reuse the same asset with independent pose state. The actual posed vertex bounds, rather than a skinned mesh's cached bounding box, verify the unit-length contract.

Summoning uses a low tonal pressure bend and restrained water Foley; expiry adds a brief lower pressure release. Existing recorded fish/human feeding sounds are used by the companion's actual meals. Music, voice recordings and other skills remain unchanged. New sounds use the existing bounded voice graph, pause/reset and mute paths.

## Cost and audio measurements

The original asset has ten mesh draws, 18,358 triangles and five shared materials. Player, Guide and companion share geometry/materials but have independent skeleton and animation state. One reusable companion is a bounded extra visible actor; it is not claimed to be free.

A serial 120-frame A→B→A observation on an Apple M2 Pro, Chrome 154, ANGLE Metal, 1440×900 / DPR 1, High quality, 25 m Zombie Shark in Europa measured 54.50 → 60.31 → 58.08 FPS with the companion absent → present → absent. Mean companion-update CPU time was 0.0033 → 0.0392 → 0.0033 ms/frame. This live scene has moving ecology and short warm-up noise; it establishes neither a FPS improvement nor a thermal guarantee. An earlier headless run was substantially slower (14.24 → 12.58 → 14.62 FPS) and remains preserved rather than treated as an equivalent hardware-rendering sample.

After warm-up, eight summon/reset cycles held renderer resource counts at 364 geometries and 161 textures. The first summon adds its private live skeleton/skinned resources and activates existing effect resources; subsequent casts reuse them. This short resource plateau is not proof of an entire round's memory or power behavior.

Actual offline game audio graphs at 22.05 kHz rendered two new effects: summoning peak 0.106 / RMS 0.0148; corpse burst peak 0.0656 / RMS 0.00856. Both have zero clipped or non-finite samples and release all three transient voices. These measurements validate output/lifecycle, not subjective listening quality. Existing performed fish/human feeding recordings remain unchanged.

Physical-device control feel, sustained heat/energy, natural full-round balance and human listening review remain acceptance boundaries. Controlled browser setup and clock-boundary probes do not establish natural 60-second survival or thermal performance.

## Distributed damage and predatory face follow-up

The user rejected the first appearance: damage was concentrated in a neat abdominal opening and the rounded eye looked vacant. The follow-up replaces the oval rims with interrupted, irregular tissue edges. Separate tears expose cartilage around the skull and gills, dorsal vertebrae and tail peduncle, while retaining the continuous shark silhouette. Dried maroon and fresher crimson staining follows the wound boundaries, waterward smears and shredded fins through shared vertex colors, without luminous red materials. The narrower amber eyes sit under lowered brows; fitted skull rails, cheek contours and a tenser jaw replace the raised round-eye expression. This remains an original fantasy animal.

Player, companion and Guide use this same revision. The local -Z direction, unit-length scale, jaw-attached feeding anchor and solid-body rules remain unchanged. The resting jaw opens slightly further; physical feeding still follows the animated mouth. No sacrifice, companion eligibility, nutrition, growth, ecology, audio or regional rule changes were made. Both-language appearance notes are now actually displayed in the Guide, rather than only stored on the catalogue entry.

Fresh validation for this art follow-up is separate from the earlier feature evidence above: 32 focused character, player-motion, feeding-transition and Guide unit checks pass, including full-cycle posed vertices at 3/15/30 m, independent animation/cache, real swept-mouth contact and occlusion. Three real development views (English 1440×900, Chinese 390×667 touch and English 320×568 touch) pass native selection, summon, pause, expiry and reset; a controlled legal sunfish is caught through the real companion AI and credits one owner meal, followed by swallow cleanup. Four native Guide angles and in-ocean follow-camera views expose the distributed damage. Five controlled keyframes through the actual Guide renderer additionally show cruise, both sprint extremes, peak feeding and jaw recovery; these are presentation checks, not natural encounter footage. The model now has 28,210 triangles, ten draws and five shared materials, compared with the rejected asset's 18,358 triangles. A serial headed Chrome 154 / ANGLE Metal / Apple M2 Pro, 1440×900 DPR 1, High, 25 m Europa sample measured 60.16 → 60.18 → 60.05 FPS with companion absent → present → absent across 120 frames per stage; p95 was 18.6/18.7/18.8 ms. These are bounded desktop observations, not a before/after improvement or a physical-phone heat guarantee.
