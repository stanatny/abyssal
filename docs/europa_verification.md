## Initial implemented scope

Europa is an enclosed fantasy ice ocean with five connected habitats, a solid full-body roof, floor-matched terrain, continuous collidable arches, suspended biological colonies, sulfide vents and a wide lord basin. Its surface capability bypasses Earth sky, fleet and bird construction. Both existing playable characters retain shared movement, nutrition, growth, hunger and skills. Actual steep roof impact mirrors the accepted ground recovery; ordinary free-water heading is preserved.

Sixteen original ordinary kinds instantiate 247 organisms. Guide classification is separate from nutrition/predator tiers; alien grazers, hunters, giants and residents share habitat-aware movement and normal respawn. The initial audit caught two ordinary hunters using the reserved lord tier and some threat anchors too far from feeding routes. Ordinary tiers and those Europa-only anchors were corrected, with an actual-consumption regression for every ordinary kind. Earth ecology arrays and shared survival values are unchanged.

The 58 m Abyss Weaver has a continuous three-lobed mantle, roughly 85 m extended span and six bifurcating articulated arms. Its three Tidal Loom sectors use the same angles, height, radius and sweep clock for warning geometry and damage. Validated hits respect cover/invulnerability and settle once per attack. Escape gaps and vertical clearance remain; three separate real flank contacts defeat the one persistent local lord. Completion requires 30 m and that defeat.

The five-region menu, Alien Life filter, descriptions, role labels, rewards, radar, objective and records support English and Simplified Chinese. Europa has an independent exploration/pursuit/lord score in the existing audio context and bounded voice graph. No downloaded reference picture or third-party sci-fi model is bundled; designs are original and explicitly imaginary, not discovered extraterrestrial life. Avatar was not used.

Stock is the sum of nutrition that could be obtained, not guaranteed capture throughput or a full-round balance claim. An unrefueled automated keyboard route completed both Orca directions and Squid descent; Squid died from starvation on its straight return, without a collision stall. ## Rendering, audio and lifecycle

Reuse existing geometry/material caches, static merging, instancing, spatial visibility, staged scene preparation and the main clock. No per-creature light or private animation loop was added. Three pooled point lamps follow nearby colony anchors; small emission fades with distance. GPU colony motion and bounded thermal plumes reuse one time uniform. Floor batches and other construction stages yield real work, preserve cancellation cleanup and loading rollback.

`switch_plateau.json` records twenty real switches across all five regions. By the third/fourth cycles each region's geometry and texture counts match exactly; Europa stabilizes at 523 geometries and 457 textures in this headless lifecycle probe. Counts differ from headed samples because viewport/quality/presentation conditions differ; the within-probe plateau is the evidence. `rollback_report.json` injects one compile failure, operates the visible Return to Home button, preserves Mariana's 333 ordinary organisms and then successfully retries Europa with 247 and the ice capability. The initial probe incorrectly waited for that failure dialog to dismiss itself; its timeout is retained separately, not treated as a game failure or a successful rollback.

Measured model cost (`model_costs.json`) ranges from 888 to 6144 triangles per ordinary model. Most ordinary kinds render in 3–13 parts; Rift Reaver uses 15, Prism Hunter 19 and the lord 28 articulated/static batches. These exceed some initial draw-part planning targets, so aggregate populated-view cost is also measured rather than hiding animated parts to meet a count. Boss cost is 26,216 triangles. Near-field detail and edible populations are retained.

Headed Chrome desktop samples use the current Mac, 1440 × 900, DPR 1, 25 m Orca, full live populations, 120 frames per quality at fixed coordinates. The Garden High sample is approximately 60.7 FPS, p95 18.7 ms, 137 draws and 433,746 triangles; Smooth is approximately 60.6 FPS, 118 draws and 340,692 triangles. The same-session Atlantis temple High sample is approximately 60.5 FPS, p95 19.5 ms, 255 draws and 904,996 triangles. A separate first-encounter Hollow sample measured 48 FPS High (p95 49.3 ms) and 56 FPS Smooth (p95 26.4 ms), with 115/105 draws and about 164k triangles. Its largest render submission was 87 ms; this single short sample does not identify whether first-use shader/driver work or other frame work caused that spike. It is not evidence for a steady 60 FPS lord arena. The isolated initial Europa switch in this sample took 2.17 s, with a longest observed task of 404 ms; there is still synchronous preparation/driver work despite staged construction. These different scenes demonstrate a cost envelope, not a before/after optimization gain or phone thermal result. The initial loading observer included boot work; do not attribute its maximum task to the subsequent region switch.

`audio_report.json` and `europa_music.wav` contain actual OceanAudio offline rendering of exploration → pursuit → lord → pause/mute/resume. Pursuit RMS is about 2.66 times calm; the peak is about 0.245 with no invalid samples/clipping. Pause settles to silence; mute settles below 0.0001 peak and resume produces signal. At most 19 voices were present in this probe. These establish wiring, headroom and lifecycle, not subjective listening approval. Existing accepted feeding recordings are reused; no human voice is emitted by this region.
