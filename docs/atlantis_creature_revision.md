# Atlantis creature anatomy revision

Development candidate, 2026-09-29. This revision responds to the rejected seahorse and the overly stocky swordfish, and reviews all seven regional creatures against the [asset quality standard](asset_quality_standard.md). It changes the shared procedural assets used by both the Ocean Guide and the population factory. It does not change catalog lengths, spawning, rewards, contact rules, swimming direction, or combat balance.

## Anatomy and materials

| Creature               | Revision and visible result                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Short-snouted seahorse | Reprofiled the forehead, neck, belly and short upturned snout. The trunk is laterally compressed, with small plate junctions and longitudinal ridges; the previous large accordion corrugations are removed. A low irregular coronet, fitted cheek plates, larger exposed eyes, small cheek-level pectorals and a rougher mottled brown surface replace the smooth tube appearance. The continuous curled tail retains its independent skinning. |
| Swordfish              | Reduced trunk depth and width, tapered the forehead into the flattened bill, fitted the eye and pectoral roots to the new surface, and reshaped the first dorsal into a swept crescent. The narrower lower jaw closes near the bill base. No pelvic fins or adult jaw teeth were added.                                                                                                                                                          |
| Blue shark             | Shortened the ventral mouth opening and reduced the exposed dentition; the long snout and swept pectorals remain distinct from the accepted stockier great-white asset.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Common cuttlefish      | Retracted the two feeding tentacles within the eight-arm crown, gathered the resting arm tips, added fitted W-shaped pupils, and broke up regular mantle bars into mottled camouflage. The paired skirt fins retain independent traveling waves.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Atlantic spadefish     | Blunted the previously pointed face while retaining its tall compressed body, five dark bars and separate dorsal/anal extensions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Ichthyosaur            | Set the large eyes and surrounding rim into the skull instead of leaving an obvious protruding ring. The long toothed jaws, four flippers and lateral tail movement remain.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Helicoprion            | Tapered the lower jaw tip and removed the artificial white lower-tail lobe. The stationary tooth whorl remains seated on the lower-jaw midline and moves only with the jaw. The whole-body reconstruction remains qualified.                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

All four axial predators now use related dorsal/side colors across the vertical caudal fin instead of painting its entire lower lobe white. The color change preserves ventral countershading on the body.

References checked during this revision: [MarLIN short-snouted seahorse](https://www.marlin.ac.uk/species/detail/1788), [Florida Museum swordfish](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/swordfish/), [blue shark](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/blue-shark/), [Atlantic spadefish](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/atlantic-spadefish/), [Monterey Bay Aquarium common cuttlefish](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals-the-ocean/animals-a-to-z/common-cuttlefish), [Natural History Museum ichthyosaur](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/what-is-an-ichthyosaur.html), and the [Idaho State University Helicoprion research account](https://www.isu.edu/news/2013-spring/idaho-state-university-researchers-solve-mysteries-of-ancient-shark-w-spiral-toothed-jaw-results-published-in-royal-societys-journal-biology-letters.html). Biological qualifications remain in [ecology references](atlantis_ecology_sources.md).

The MarLIN photograph by Sue Daly was inspected as a visual reference; it is copyrighted, retained only in ignored local evidence, and is not included as a game texture or distributed asset. All revised geometry and colors are original procedural artwork. This is a stylized reconstruction, not a scientific scan.

## Checks and evidence

The accepted boxfish, great white and pliosaur were rendered in the same inspection viewport as category comparators (`baseline_boxfish.png`, `baseline_shark.png`, `baseline_pliosaur.png`). Their attached structures, distinct volumes, fitted eyes, material variation and shared geometry establish the comparison; the rejected first-pass Atlantis assets are preserved only as before-images.

New checks run:

- `node --test tests/creature_atlantis.test.js tests/creature_collision.test.js`: **27 passed**. These cover finite buffers, real posed vertices, measured catalog lengths, centered bounds, shared immutable geometry/materials, independent skeletons, complete 16-second cycles at three effort levels, exposed pupils, and collision regressions. Added a slender-torso regression for swordfish and retracted-tentacle bounds for cuttlefish. The pupil visibility ray now samples the actual W-shaped pupil rather than its empty bounding-box center.
- All seven assets inspected from side, front, three-quarter and rear views. No separated tail seams, detached fin roots or exposed skeletal-ring artifacts were seen in the reviewed views.
- Seventeen rendered animation samples per creature cover 0–16 seconds, with a cruise-to-high-effort transition at 8 seconds. Six-frame contact sheets make the slow body and tail changes reviewable; the source samples also show the faster fin phases. Existing normalized direction and independent root transforms are retained.
- Actual English Ocean Guide captures at **1440×900** and **390×667** show all seven assets. The model dimensions and UI descriptions continue to use the shared catalog; seahorse remains **0.15 m in upright height**.
- Actual ocean captures retain the real scene, lighting, fog and naturally spawned instance scales. For inspection, the player was relocated near an existing animal, the game was paused, and the camera was placed at close and more distant views. These are controlled camera captures, not natural encounter or feeding evidence. The 0.15 m seahorse is intentionally small at three meters and its fine plates are not readable at that distance.
- Guide/animation capture reported **zero page errors**. No frame-rate or physical-phone claim is made from these runs.

Evidence is under ignored `.local/creature_revision/`: `guide_1440_*.png`, `guide_390_*.png`, `*_rear.png`, `*_motion_sheet.png`, `motion/`, `ocean_*_close.png`, `ocean_*_distance.png`, `browser.json`, `ocean.json`, and `final_tests.log`. Side/front/three-quarter inspection frames are in `.local/atlantis_models/`; the pre-revision frames are copied under `creature_revision/before/`. `capture_revision.mjs` and `capture_ocean.mjs` preserve the capture methods. These are actual model renders and game captures, not generated concept art.
