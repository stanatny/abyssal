# Bermuda art and audio notes

All Bermuda assets were authored independently for this candidate. No delegated delivery, external model, movie texture or downloaded music is included. Existing accepted character, lord, shared-creature and Foley assets retain their existing attribution. New geometry, procedural surfaces and score source remain editable in this repository.

## Environment

`bermuda_terrain.js` is the authoritative seabed sampler; `bermuda_ocean.js` builds the matching mesh, GPU water, instanced stones/corals and bounded nearest-pearl lighting. The mesh refines only the support-bed transition and deep floor clamp, selecting diagonals along real height folds. The slate cloud dome and falling rain line segments are procedural shaders. Seven tapered, twisting waterspouts have moving cloud striation and foam skirts. Short lightning pulses and thunder use the shared world/audio lifecycle; reduced motion suppresses flashes.

`bermuda_wreck.js` uses continuous hull lofts and segmented sloping plates, with real openings, intermediate decks and a connected atrium. Wood and metal retain accepted fine surface detail. The central route stays clear; furnished side bays contain tables, bedsteads, strapped cargo chests and shelves. Cargo, exposed framing, staircases, machinery, portholes and four funnels provide scale. Coral and pearl growth are restrained navigation cues. This is an original fictional wreck inspired by ocean-liner construction, not a claim to reconstruct Titanic's exact historical layout.

`bermuda_fleet.js` owns the platform, freighter, tanker and original Flying Dutchman. `bermuda_dutchman.js` and `bermuda_davy_jones.js` provide its curved hull, two gun decks, three stern galleries, ragged sail shader and original captain. Platform legs sample the actual seabed; railings, ladder, work cabin, helipad, pipe bank, derrick and crane establish an industrial silhouette. The phantom vessel uses a layered timber hull, stern galleries, carved bow, rigging, deck planks, cannons and animated torn cloth. Reference study used the [Disney D23 practical model](https://d23.com/pirates-of-the-caribbean-flying-dutchman-models/), [Lauren Polizzi set drawings](https://laurenpolizzi.com/set-design/pirates-of-the-carribean-k6msp), [official character reference](https://pirates.disney.com/characters) and [ILM production overview](https://www.ilm.com/vfx/pirates-of-the-caribbean-dead-mans-chest-2/). No reference image, film mesh or texture is bundled. Its warning ring, non-homing volleys and solid-cover casts form a reviewable gameplay hazard.

`bermuda_biomes.js` supplies reef arches, fractured spires, aircraft/cargo wrecks, hydrothermal communities and marine snow. `bermuda_reef.js` supplies shared branching coral colonies with tapered pale growing tips; sea fans reuse the accepted reticulated geometry instead of radial sticks. The nursery remains navigable.

Static landmark geometry is merged by material. The ocean owns three local pearl lights, while work lights are emissive surfaces. Do not add a point light to every window, pearl or creature. Map-owned resources have idempotent disposal; immutable model caches are separate from instance state.

## Creature anatomy and references

The revision replaces isolated eyeballs and simple flat appendages with profile-attached eye sockets, opercula/gill grooves, nostrils, sculpted opposing jaws, fin roots and body-specific procedural markings. Livyatan has a capped massive head, jaw seam and opposing tooth rows; Cameroceras has shell growth bands and a fuller curling arm crown. Fin/tail pivots follow the swimming skeleton instead of separating from it.

`creature_bermuda.js` creates distinct continuous bodies, closed sculpted fins, countershading/markings, eyes, gills and jaws. Fin membranes have procedural ray detail. Axial swimming bones and fins have instance-local movement; Cameroceras uses tapered arm motion, while Livyatan uses vertical stalk/fluke motion. Roots retain the shared normalized length and travel/contact convention. Modern color/anatomy references inform the silhouettes; gameplay aggression, speed, nutrition and depth bands are adaptations.

## Original soundtrack

`music_bermuda.js` defines an original 46 BPM exploration score with eight 32-step harmonic sections. A step is half a beat, so one harmonic cycle is approximately 167 seconds. Long detuned low strings, slow friction/pressure noise and a sparse inharmonic metal echo replace the earlier regular bright bell motif. Semitone/tritone movement creates unease. A separate 118 BPM ostinato enters promptly for pursuit; the guardian bus adds bass/noise pressure during a lord encounter. Shared pursuit/return/fade state determines transitions, rather than inventing a separate regional AI.

All voices, filtering, depth muffling, mute, pause and map changes reuse `OceanAudio` and its bounded buses. Thunder, ghost warning and broadside transients are original short procedural effects. Accepted fish intake/bite/bubble Foley remains unchanged. There is one existing AudioContext, no hidden autoplay and no private audio loop.

Native offline renders and live AudioWorklet recordings are stored under ignored `bermuda_audio/`. See the verification record for waveform, voice, contrast and entry measurements. These establish functioning mix/lifecycle, not subjective musical acceptance; real headphones/speakers and user listening remain necessary.
